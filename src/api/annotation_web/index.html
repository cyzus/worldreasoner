Event-source validation study

You are helping evaluate the reliability of source-backed event records used to assess forecasting systems. The research is conducted by the research team.

What you will do

You will review event claims. For each item, answer: Does the cited article support the full event claim shown above? You are not being asked to forecast an outcome or determine whether an event caused it.

Study procedure

Read the complete event claim and supplied article, then judge entity match, event-date validity, and source support. Provide one supporting excerpt and explain every rejected or qualified judgment. Do not use outside sources.

Information collected and research use

  • Your Prolific ID, annotation choices, excerpts, explanations, response timing, and quality-check results are recorded.
  • Your Prolific ID is used to administer and resume the study. Research analysis and reporting will use pseudonymized records and aggregate results.
  • Progress is autosaved. Unsubmitted records are retained for resumption but excluded from completed-study analysis.

Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participation is voluntary. You may stop before final submission by returning the study on Prolific. You may contact the research team to request deletion of an unfinished record. Payment and approval follow the duration, reward, and completion terms shown on Prolific.

Confidentiality and contact

This interface does not ask for your name or personal contact details. Questions about the study or a withdrawal request should be sent through Prolific messaging, or to research@example.org. Do not include your annotation answers in email.

Content notice

Articles may discuss politics, conflict, disasters, illness, death, or other sensitive real-world events. You may stop and return the study if you do not wish to continue.

Important instructions

  • Judge the full claim, including dates, entities, and qualifiers. The short title is only a label.
  • Publication date alone does not prove when an event occurred.
  • This study requires your own judgment. AI-assisted annotation is not permitted.

How to judge an item

The full event claim is always the target. Compare every material detail with the cited article.

Rubric and examples
Entity match
  • Correct: same people, organisations, places, and products.
  • Ambiguous: the article does not resolve whether they are the same.
  • Incorrect: the article concerns a different entity.
Event-date validity
  • Correct: the claimed occurrence date is supported.
  • Near match: only a one- or two-day timezone or reporting difference.
  • Incorrect: the article supports a different date; enter it below.
  • Unclear: the article cannot establish the occurrence date.
Source support
  • Full: all material details are supported.
  • Partial: only part of the claim is supported.
  • None: the event is not mentioned.
  • Contradictory: the article directly conflicts with the claim.

Example: If the same council and policy appear but the article says 14 May instead of 12 May, choose Correct entity, Incorrect date (14 May), and Partial support.
